# Text-to-speech script for the weeBell rotary phone
#
# Everything the operator says, as input for the text-to-speech service. The behavior behind each clip is in
# doc/phone_ui.md; the clip format, banks and recording notes are in doc/audio_clips.md.
#
# FORMAT
#   #            A line starting with # is a comment and is not sent to the service.
#   @key value   A directive. It applies to every clip line after it, until the next @persona.
#                  @persona <name>   starts a persona section (one per era: era1, era2, era3); clip IDs are unique
#                                    within a persona
#                  @voice <name>     the service's voice for this persona (-- until chosen)
#                  @style <text>     delivery direction for the voice (not spoken); repeated @style lines are joined
#   id: text     A clip. The id is everything before the first ": "; the text after it is sent to the service.
#                The full clip name is <persona>/<id>, for example era1/op_list.
#   --           Text of just "--" means the wording is completely unclear; the clip is skipped until it is written.
#   [sighs]      A sound effect in square brackets, for services that support audio tags.
#   @pause <s>   Only in a hold scene: <s> seconds of silence (or music only, in era 2) to leave after the clip above
#                when the scene is assembled in Audacity. Not sent to the service.
#
# Blank lines are ignored.
#
# NOTES
# - Every persona has the same clip IDs in the same order, so a missing line is easy to spot.
# - Confirmations are generated as whole sentences, one per value (for example "Earpiece volume, seven."), rather than
#   joined from a label clip and a digit clip; there is room on the card and in the budget, and whole sentences sound
#   natural.
# - Numbers are written as words. The digit 0 is spoken the way each persona's era would have said it, not as a fixed
#   rule: the 1940s-50s US operators say "operator" or "oh" (the 0 hole on a US dial was labelled Operator); the UK
#   operator says "oh" (on UK dials the letter O shares the 0 hole) and "nought" only for a quantity of nothing; the
#   1965+ Bell System recording says "zero" (not verified; review). The same applies to any digits read in a number,
#   for example "three five seven oh nine".
# - One operator (persona) per era, each with its own voice, chosen by audition in the text-to-speech app: era1 the
#   harried 1950s US switchboard operator, era2 the Bell System recording, era3 the GPO operator. The era set in mode 5
#   decides who speaks.
# - The tone-only mode without an SD card has no clips, the dialed number is never read back, and the microphone
#   volume is only confirmed, never sampled by recording and playing back.
# - Timing (seconds before each waiting remark, and between the no-phone steps) lives in the firmware, not here. The
#   exception is the hold scenes: their pauses are part of the recording, assembled by hand.
# - Hold scenes (hold_01, hold_02, ... with @pause lines) are generated clip by clip, then joined in Audacity with the
#   given pauses into one clip per persona, hold_scene, about 160 to 170 s long. It plays during the pairing window, right
#   after pair_start; pairing success cuts it off (pair_paired breaks in), and the window's timeout follows its last
#   line. Speech lengths in the comments are estimates; adjust the pauses so the total stays under the 180 s window.

# --- No phone connected ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Played instead of dial tone when the handset is lifted and no cellphone is connected (doc/phone_ui.md).
# nophone_intro: no phone has been paired; explains pairing. nophone_intro_away: a phone is paired but cannot be
#   reached (out of range, or Bluetooth off).
# nophone_1 / nophone_1_away: the first follow-up if nothing is dialed, asking about pairing (or the missing phone).
# nophone_2 to nophone_6: later steps while nothing is dialed; confused, then lonely, never truly dark.
# nophone_final: after nophone_6, just before the era's off-hook treatment (dial tone until hang-up in eras 1 and 3;
#   the off-hook howler in era 2).
# nophone_glad: a digit is dialed after the lonely steps (nophone_4 on); plays before whatever the digit does.
# nophone_call: the caller dialed 1 (place a call), which cannot work without a phone. The monologue then restarts.
# nophone_back: a mode finished and the phone is still not connected; the monologue restarts after this line.
# nophone_connected: the phone connects during the monologue; then dial tone.
nophone_intro: Operator. No phone hooked up. Dial two and pick Kellogg Switchboard on your phone. Let's go.
nophone_intro_away: Operator. Can't raise your phone, pal. Bluetooth on? I'll keep ringing it.
nophone_1: Hey. You gonna pair a phone or what? Dial two.
nophone_1_away: Still can't raise your phone. It in the other room or what?
nophone_2: Hello? Anybody on this line?
nophone_3: Quiet in here. Too quiet.
nophone_4: Whole board's dark. Just me and the plugs.
nophone_5: You still there? Didn't think so.
nophone_6: Guess I'll take my break, then.
nophone_final: Putting you back on dial tone, pal. Holler if you need me.
nophone_glad: Oh! Somebody's there. Okay, okay.
nophone_call: Call who? On what? You got no phone hooked up. Dial two.
nophone_back: So. About that phone.
nophone_connected: There's your phone. Go ahead.

# --- Call errors (mode 1) ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# doc/phone_ui.md, "Call errors". Remaining digits are ignored; after the clip, dial tone.
# call_bad_area: the area code is impossible (starts with 0 or 1, or is an N11 code such as 411).
# call_bad_exchange: the exchange (digits 4 to 6) starts with 0 or 1.
# call_blocked: the area code is blocked (900 or 976, premium rate).
# call_refused: the cellphone refused to dial, for a reason other than no service (that uses noservice_call).
# Era 1: spoken as the intercept operator.
call_bad_area: What number you calling? No such area code, pal. Check it and dial again.
call_bad_exchange: That number's no good, pal. Check it and dial again.
call_blocked: Pay-by-the-minute numbers? Not on my board, pal.
call_refused: Your phone won't take the call, pal. Try again.

# --- Connection status --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Follows what the Bluetooth stack reports (doc/phone_ui.md, "Connection status"). Lines marked "breaks in" cut off
# whatever clip is playing, like a dialing interruption.
# conn_try: pick-up with a paired phone that is not connected; a reconnect attempt starts.
# conn_try_ok: that attempt succeeded; then dial tone. (If it fails, nophone_intro_away follows.)
# conn_ok_interrupt: breaks in when the phone connects while another clip is playing; then dial tone. (During a pause in
#   the no-phone monologue, nophone_connected is used instead.)
# conn_lost: the connection dropped while off-hook, or during a call; then the no-phone monologue (away version).
# pair_paired: breaks in (or follows pair_start) when pairing succeeds; the phone is still connecting. pair_success
#   follows once it is ready.
# pair_noconn: paired, but the phone did not connect within 15 s; then the no-phone monologue (away version).
# noservice: at pick-up, the connected cellphone reports no network service; then dial tone.
# noservice_call: the caller dialed 1 while there is no service; then dial tone.
conn_try: Hang on, ringing your phone.
conn_try_ok: Got it. Go ahead.
conn_ok_interrupt: Wait, got it! You're connected.
conn_lost: Lost your phone, pal.
pair_paired: Paired! Hang on, it's connecting.
pair_noconn: Paired, but it won't hook up. Bluetooth still on?
noservice: Your phone's got no service, pal. No calls till it does.
noservice_call: Still no service. Try later.

# conn_ok_interrupt: breaks in when the phone connects while another clip is playing; then dial tone. (During a pause in
#   the no-phone monologue, nophone_connected is used instead.)
# conn_lost: the connection dropped while off-hook, or during a call; then the no-phone monologue (away version).
# pair_paired: breaks in (or follows pair_start) when pairing succeeds; the phone is still connecting. pair_success

# noservice_call: the caller dialed 1 while there is no service; then dial tone.
conn_try: Please wait while this recording attempts to reach your telephone.
conn_try_ok: Your telephone is now connected.
conn_ok_interrupt: Correction: your telephone is now connected.
conn_lost: We're sorry. The connection to your telephone has been lost.
pair_paired: Pairing is complete. Please wait while your telephone connects.
pair_noconn: We're sorry. Your telephone has been paired but has not connected. Please check that its Bluetooth is turned on.
noservice: We're sorry. Your telephone is not receiving service at this time.
noservice_call: We're sorry. Your call cannot be completed. Your telephone has no service.

# --- Call errors (mode 1) ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# doc/phone_ui.md, "Call errors". Remaining digits are ignored; after the clip, dial tone.
# call_bad_area: the area code is impossible (starts with 0 or 1, or is an N11 code such as 411).
# call_bad_exchange: the exchange (digits 4 to 6) starts with 0 or 1.
# call_blocked: the area code is blocked (900 or 976, premium rate).
# call_refused: the cellphone refused to dial, for a reason other than no service (that uses noservice_call).

call_blocked: I'm afraid calls to that code are not permitted from this telephone, caller.
call_refused: I'm afraid your handset declined to place the call, caller. Do try again shortly.
