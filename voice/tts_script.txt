# Text-to-speech script for the weeBell rotary phone
#
# Everything the operator says, as input for the text-to-speech service. The behavior behind each clip is in
# doc/phone_ui.md; the clip format, banks and recording notes are in doc/audio_clips.md.
#
# FORMAT
#   #            A line starting with # is a comment and is not sent to the service.
#   @key value   A directive. It applies to every clip line after it, until the next @persona.
#                  @persona <name>   starts a persona section; clip IDs are unique within a persona
#                  @voice <name>     the service's voice for this persona (-- until chosen)
#                  @style <text>     delivery direction for the voice (not spoken); repeated @style lines are joined
#   id: text     A clip. The id is everything before the first ": "; the text after it is sent to the service.
#                The full clip name is <persona>/<id>, for example us1_polite/op_intro.
#   --           Text of just "--" means the wording is completely unclear; the clip is skipped until it is written.
#   [sighs]      A sound effect in square brackets, for services that support audio tags.
#
# Blank lines are ignored.
#
# NOTES
# - Every persona has the same clip IDs in the same order, so a missing line is easy to spot.
# - Confirmations are generated as whole sentences, one per value (for example "Earpiece volume, seven."), rather than
#   joined from a label clip and a digit clip; there is room on the card and in the budget, and whole sentences sound
#   natural.
# - Numbers are written as words. The digit 0 is spoken the way each persona's era would have said it, not as a fixed
#   rule: the 1940s-50s US operators say "operator" or "oh" (the 0 hole on a US dial was labelled Operator); the UK
#   operator says "oh" (on UK dials the letter O shares the 0 hole) and "nought" only for a quantity of nothing; the
#   1965+ Bell System recording says "zero" (not verified; review). The same applies to any digits read in a number,
#   for example "three five seven oh nine".
# - Which persona speaks is set by the era and personality (doc/phone_ui.md, open issue 34). Era 1 has two candidate
#   personalities; eras 2 and 3 have one each.
# - Not in this file yet: dialed-number readback (open issue 12), a microphone sample (open issue 21), and the
#   tone-only mode without an SD card (no clips by definition).
# - Timing (seconds before each waiting remark, and between the no-phone steps) lives in the firmware, not here.

# --- Operator list (dial 0) -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Played in order when the caller dials 0. One line per mode; the closing line is followed by dial tone.
op_intro: Operator. Number, please? Or perhaps I can help you.
op_mode_1: Dial one and then the ten-digit number, and I'll put your call through.
op_mode_2: Dial two to connect your pocket telephone.
op_mode_3: Dial three if you'd like to hear me better.
op_mode_4: Dial four if your party can't hear you.
op_mode_5: Dial five to change the times.
op_mode_6: Dial six, and I'll forget your telephone was ever here.
op_close: Dial operator any time you need me. Thank you, caller.

# --- Unassigned mode digit ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The caller dialed 7, 8 or 9 as the first digit, which select nothing. A witty remark, then dial tone.
err_mode_7: Seven? That line was disconnected years ago, dear. Here's your dial tone back.
err_mode_8: Eight? Nobody's lived at eight since the war, dear. Here's your dial tone back.
err_mode_9: Nine? Heavens, dear, nine is where I keep my knitting. Here's your dial tone back.

# --- Mode 2: Bluetooth pairing --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# pair_start: on entering mode 2; a discoverable window of about 3 minutes opens. "weeBell" is the Bluetooth device
#   name the cellphone shows (not final, open issue 16).
# pair_success / pair_failed / pair_timeout: the outcome, then dial tone.
# pair_invalid: the caller dialed a digit during the pairing window (no digits are needed).
pair_start: Of course. Open the Bluetooth settings on your pocket telephone and choose weeBell. I'll wait right here.
pair_success: There you are, dear. You're connected.
pair_failed: Oh, dear. That didn't take. Dial two to try again.
pair_timeout: I'm afraid I couldn't find your telephone. Dial two to try again.
pair_invalid: No need to dial, dear. Just choose me on your telephone.

# --- Mode 3: Earpiece volume ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# vol_ear_prompt: on entering mode 3, the label and the instruction.
# vol_ear_<n>: after the caller dials n (1 to 9). Played at the new level, so the whole sentence is a sample of how
#   loud calls will sound.
# vol_ear_invalid: the caller dialed 0, which is not a level (the first invalid digit; see "Repeated invalid digits").
# vol_ear_wait: the first remark if nothing is dialed (3 s by default), before the shared waiting remarks.
vol_ear_prompt: Earpiece volume. Dial one for the quietest, up to nine for the loudest.
vol_ear_1: Earpiece volume, one. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_2: Earpiece volume, two. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_3: Earpiece volume, three. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_4: Earpiece volume, four. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_5: Earpiece volume, five. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_6: Earpiece volume, six. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_7: Earpiece volume, seven. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_8: Earpiece volume, eight. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_9: Earpiece volume, nine. This is how your calls will sound.
vol_ear_invalid: That's the operator, dear, and you've already got me. One to nine, please.
vol_ear_wait: How loud would you like me, dear? Just dial a number.

# --- Mode 4: Microphone volume --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Same pattern as mode 3. The confirmation cannot be a sample of the caller's own voice (open issue 21), so it only
# confirms the level.
vol_mic_prompt: Microphone volume. Dial one for the quietest, up to nine for the loudest.
vol_mic_1: Microphone volume, one. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_2: Microphone volume, two. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_3: Microphone volume, three. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_4: Microphone volume, four. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_5: Microphone volume, five. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_6: Microphone volume, six. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_7: Microphone volume, seven. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_8: Microphone volume, eight. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_9: Microphone volume, nine. Your party will hear you at that level.
vol_mic_invalid: Oh? That's just me again, dear. One to nine, please.
vol_mic_wait: Shall I turn you up, or down, dear?

# --- Mode 5: Tone era -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# era_prompt: on entering mode 5.
# era_<n>: after the caller dials n (1 to 3): the era's number and name.
# era_invalid: the caller dialed 4 to 9 (eras that do not exist yet).
# era_invalid_0: the caller dialed 0.
# era_wait: the first remark if nothing is dialed.
era_prompt: Tone era. Dial one, two, or three.
era_1: Tone era one. The American exchange, before nineteen sixty-five.
era_2: Tone era two. The Bell System tone plan, from nineteen sixty-five.
era_3: Tone era three. The British General Post Office.
era_invalid: Oh, I don't know anything about the future, dear. One, two, or three.
era_invalid_0: Operator? You've already got me, dear. One, two, or three.
era_wait: Which era would you like, dear? One, two, or three?

# --- Mode 6: Forget pairing -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# forget_confirm: on entering mode 6; the caller must dial 6 again.
# forget_done: after the second 6; the bond is removed, then dial tone.
# forget_none: on entering mode 6 when no phone is paired (open issue 19), then dial tone.
# forget_invalid: a digit other than 6 at the confirmation.
# forget_wait: the first remark if nothing is dialed.
forget_confirm: Dial six again, and I'll forget your telephone. Hang up if you've changed your mind.
forget_done: It's forgotten, dear. Your telephone was never here.
forget_none: There's no telephone to forget, dear.
forget_invalid: That wasn't a six, dear. Dial six to forget it, or hang up to keep it.
forget_wait: Do you want me to forget this telephone or not, dear?

# --- Repeated invalid digits (shared by all modes) ------------------------------------------------------------------
# The first invalid digit in a mode gets that mode's own *_invalid remark. If the caller keeps dialing invalid digits,
# these follow in order, one per repeat; after invalid_4 it repeats. After each remark the mode waits for the same
# input again. The count resets when a valid digit is dialed or the mode ends.
invalid_2: Oh, dear. Let's try that once more, shall we?
invalid_3: Caller, I'm beginning to think you're doing this on purpose.
invalid_4: Is one of the little ones playing with the telephone again?

# --- Waiting in a mode (shared by modes 3 to 6) ---------------------------------------------------------------------
# After the mode's own *_wait remark, if still nothing is dialed. Default gaps: 5 s, 7 s, 9 s. Annoyed first, then sad.
# After wait_3 the caller hears the operator hang up, then dial tone. The hang-up is a recorded sound, sfx_hangup,
# not in this file (see doc/audio_clips.md, "Sound effects to record").
wait_1: Hello? I'm still here, caller.
wait_2: Caller, I do have other lines waiting.
wait_3: Well. I suppose nobody wants to talk to the operator. Goodbye, dear.

# --- No phone connected ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Played instead of dial tone when the handset is lifted and no cellphone is connected (doc/phone_ui.md).
# nophone_intro: no phone has been paired; explains pairing. nophone_intro_away: a phone is paired but cannot be
#   reached (out of range, or Bluetooth off).
# nophone_1 / nophone_1_away: the first follow-up if nothing is dialed, asking about pairing (or the missing phone).
# nophone_2 to nophone_6: later steps while nothing is dialed; confused, then lonely, never truly dark.
# nophone_final: after nophone_6, just before the era's off-hook treatment (dial tone until hang-up in eras 1 and 3;
#   the off-hook howler in era 2).
# nophone_glad: a digit is dialed after the lonely steps (nophone_4 on); plays before whatever the digit does.
# nophone_call: the caller dialed 1 (place a call), which cannot work without a phone. The monologue then restarts.
# nophone_back: a mode finished and the phone is still not connected; the monologue restarts after this line.
# nophone_connected: the phone connects during the monologue; then dial tone.
nophone_intro: Operator. I'm not connected to a telephone just yet. Dial two, and choose weeBell in your pocket telephone's Bluetooth settings.
nophone_intro_away: Operator. I can't seem to reach your pocket telephone, dear. Is it nearby, with its Bluetooth turned on? I'll keep trying.
nophone_1: Hello? Did you want to connect a telephone, dear? Just dial two.
nophone_1_away: Hello? Is your telephone nearby, dear? I'm still trying to reach it.
nophone_2: Is anyone there?
nophone_3: It's awfully quiet on this line.
nophone_4: I do get lonely in here, you know. Not a single call all day.
nophone_5: Hello? Anyone at all?
nophone_6: I'll just wait here, then. I'm very good at waiting.
nophone_final: I'll leave the dial tone on for you, dear. Just dial operator if you need me.
nophone_glad: Oh! Someone's there! How lovely.
nophone_call: I'd love to put a call through, dear, but there's no telephone to put it through. Dial two to connect one.
nophone_back: Now, dear, about that telephone.
nophone_connected: Oh, there's your telephone now. Go right ahead.

# --- Connection status --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Follows what the Bluetooth stack reports (doc/phone_ui.md, "Connection status"). Lines marked "breaks in" cut off
# whatever clip is playing, like a dialing interruption.
# conn_try: pick-up with a paired phone that is not connected; a reconnect attempt starts.
# conn_try_ok: that attempt succeeded; then dial tone. (If it fails, nophone_intro_away follows.)
# conn_ok_interrupt: breaks in when the phone connects while another clip is playing; then dial tone. (During a pause in
#   the no-phone monologue, nophone_connected is used instead.)
# conn_lost: the connection dropped while off-hook, or during a call; then the no-phone monologue (away version).
# pair_paired: breaks in (or follows pair_start) when pairing succeeds; the phone is still connecting. pair_success
#   follows once it is ready.
# pair_noconn: paired, but the phone did not connect within 15 s; then the no-phone monologue (away version).
# noservice: at pick-up, the connected cellphone reports no network service; then dial tone.
# noservice_call: the caller dialed 1 while there is no service; then dial tone.
conn_try: Let me try your pocket telephone, dear.
conn_try_ok: There you are. Go right ahead.
conn_ok_interrupt: Oh! There it is. You're connected, dear.
conn_lost: Oh, dear. I've lost your telephone.
pair_paired: Oh, it's paired! Just a moment while it connects.
pair_noconn: It's paired, dear, but it won't connect. Is its Bluetooth still turned on?
noservice: Your pocket telephone has no service just now, dear. I can't put calls through until it does.
noservice_call: I'm sorry, dear, your telephone still has no service. Try again in a little while.

# --- Interruptions --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
# The caller starts dialing while the operator is speaking. Plays at once, mixed with the dial clicks; the digit still
# works as normal.
# intr_1 to intr_3: picked at random.
# intr_esc_1 to intr_esc_3: used instead if the caller keeps interrupting, one step per repeat (open issue 37).
intr_1: One moment, please.
intr_2: I'm still speaking, dear.
intr_3: Patience, caller.
intr_esc_1: One moment, please.
intr_esc_2: Caller, I am trying to help you.
intr_esc_3: [sighs]
